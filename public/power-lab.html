Moon power lab

Will your lander survive the Moon night? Type your solar panels, the power you use at night and your battery. The lab tells you how much battery is missing and what it costs to send. The starting numbers are only examples.

For the four real landing points the night length is the full night in Nov–Dec 2026 on flat ground (D, checked against NASA JPL Horizons).

Directions: 0 = north, 90 = east, 180 = south, 270 = west. Tilt: 0 = flat, 90 = upright. Type the Sun direction yourself.
In "Both" mode, "Panel area" is the flat panels. Standing panels fixed on one side face the Sun only part of the time. Type 100 for the best case.
143 Wh/kg is the LuSEE-Night battery (S). Pick "I will type my own" if your battery is different (A).
Don't know the angle? Type the hill's height above the lander and its distance, both in metres. The lab works out the angle: atan(height ÷ distance).
Other losses: leave 0 if you have no number. Horizon angle: how many degrees the hills around the site rise above flat ground (0 = flat). If the Sun is lower than this angle, the panels get 0 W.
About $1.2 million per kg is NASA’s own estimate for future CLPS missions (S: NASA Inspector General, IG-24-013, 2024, p. 25). Type your own price if you have one.

  • DPanel power = 1361 W/m² × area × cell efficiency × (1 − other losses) × angle. Flat panel: angle = sin(Sun height). Panel facing the Sun: angle = 1.
