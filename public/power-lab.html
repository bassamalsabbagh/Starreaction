Moon power lab

A simplified teaching model of two questions every lander faces: how many watts does the panel give, and how heavy a battery gets it through the night. It is not a real site.

Nights for the four real points (IM-1, IM-2, Chandrayaan-3, Blue Ghost) come from NASA JPL Horizons exports, flat horizon, 13 Nov to 13 Dec 2026 (D). The windows are in the paper, Table III.

Directions are degrees clockwise from north (0 = north, 90 = east, 180 = south, 270 = west). Tilt: 0 = flat, 90 = vertical. Type the Sun direction yourself; we do not have it for every site.
In "Both" mode, "Panel area" is the FLAT area. 100% for standing panels is the best case: fixed panels on one side face the Sun only part of the day. Use your own estimate or put 100 for an upper bound.
Other losses: dust, heat, cell ageing together. Leave 0 if you have no number. Horizon height: how high the hills around the site rise, in degrees. 0 means a flat horizon. This number comes from Mahmoud (LOLA); when the Sun is lower than it, the panels get nothing.

Every night has a price in kilograms

On the Moon, the night is paid for in battery. Longer night, heavier battery. Rows are sorted from cheapest to most expensive.

Cost to carry it = battery kg x the cost of landing 1 kg. This counts only the weight of the battery, not what the battery itself costs to build, so it is a rough size of the problem (A). For scale, NASA delivery contracts carry roughly 80 to 130 kg of payload each (news reports, S*), so a battery of 168 kg or more (the 100 W case) does not fit in them at all.

Short night, best polar spot and average Moon night are general benchmarks. The four real-site rows are the full nights of each landing point (flat horizon, 2026), from our engine checked against NASA JPL Horizons exports (D).

Kilograms = load x hours / 143 Wh per kg, so it is only as good as the load assumption.

  • DPanel power = 1361 W/m² x area x 30% cells x angle factor. Flat panel: sin(Sun height). Standing panel facing the Sun: 1.
  • DArea needed = power needed / (1361 x 0.30 x angle factor). Battery kg = heater W x night hours / 143 Wh/kg.
  • S1361 W/m² total solar irradiance, measured by NASA SORCE and TSIS-1 (1361.6 ± 0.3): NASA Goddard, Solar Irradiance Science. It varies about 0.1% over the solar cycle, so we use one average value.
  • S30% nominal cell efficiency: NASA Small Spacecraft Technology, Power.
  • S143 Wh/kg: LuSEE-Night battery, 7,160 Wh for 50 kg (arXiv 2407.07173).
  • S43 h of darkness at the best polar spots: LROC.
  • DAbout 354 h of night is half of the 29.53 day Moon day (NASA NSSDC).
  • DBlue Ghost Sun height 3.4° is a team calculation, not a published value.
  • AIgnores shadows, panel losses and battery depth of discharge, so the battery figure is a minimum.
