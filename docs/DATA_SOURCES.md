# Data Sources

Every fact, coordinate, date, lander specification and mission outcome shown in the app must trace
back to an entry in this file. **No entry, no data.**

## Source priority

1. **NASA primary sources:** nasa.gov mission pages, NASA press releases, NASA technical reports
   (NTRS), NASA Science, NASA PDS (Planetary Data System), LRO mission data.
2. **Other government and agency science sources:** USGS Astrogeology, other space agencies'
   official pages.
3. **CLPS provider official sources:** provider press releases and mission pages (e.g. Intuitive
   Machines, Astrobotic, Firefly). Treat performance claims as the provider's own statements.
4. **Peer-reviewed literature.**
5. **Reputable journalism:** only for context, and only when 1–4 are unavailable. Flag it as such.

Never cite: Wikipedia as a primary source (it is acceptable for finding primary sources), forums,
social media posts without official provenance, or AI-generated summaries.

## How to add a source

1. Give it the next ID (`SRC-001`, `SRC-002`, …).
2. Fill in every column. The access date is the date someone actually opened the URL.
3. Reference the ID from the data record (`sourceIds: ['SRC-001']`).
4. If sources conflict, record both and note the conflict in the **Conflicts and uncertainty**
   section below.

**Map layer encoding** (`scripts/data/make_map_layers.py`, `src/data/layers/index.json`): value
tiles are 256 × 256, little-endian, row 0 at the top, in `public/data/layers/<layer>/values/<level>/`.
Elevation: int16, metres, signed, value = stored × 1 m + 0 m, NoData −32768. Slope: uint16,
degrees = stored × 0.01, NoData 65535. A stored 0 is a real 0. Levels: `base` = 160 m, each value
the mean of the 4 × 4 underlying 40 m pixels that hold data, across 80–90° S; `im1`, `im2` = the
original 40 m pixels in a 40.96 km square (1024 × 1024) around each site. Display images: the
colour TIFF's own pixels (nearest, never blended) reprojected to longitude/latitude, four quarters
of 2048 × 2048 for 80–90° S (512 px previews) and one image per patch (transparent outside its
square). Checked against the originals in docs/LAYER_CHECK.md.

- WMS 1.3.0 "WMS Lunar Server".
- CRS options: `EPSG:4326` (an Earth code, here applied to lunar data) and `IAU_2015:30100`. It was
  not verified that `IAU_2015:30100` is the Moon's IAU code.
- Layers include `LROC_WAC`, `LOLA_bw`, `LOLA_color`, `KaguyaTC_Ortho`, `LO`, `uv_v2`.
- Bounding-box longitude spans −180 to 360.
- WMS 1.3.0 with EPSG:4326 lists the bounding box latitude first. How Leaflet's WMS layer handles
  that axis order is not documented in any source found.
- Licence: AccessConstraints, Fees and ContactOrganization are absent.

### 5. USGS — LOLA DEM 118 m (SRC-007)

- Licence: "public domain"; "Please cite authors".

### 6. NASA SVS — CGI Moon Kit (SRC-009)

  - 2019 set: up to 27360 × 13680 (494.1 MB).

## Coordinate convention evidence (see KI-006)

- **USGS standard (SRC-012):** "we recommend using planetocentric" latitude, "rotated to the mean
  Earth/polar axis (ME) system", "in a -180 to 180 positive East coordinate system with a center
  longitude of 0 degrees". The vertical datum is "a reference sphere with a 1737.4 km radius".
- **The 0–360 alternative:**
  - SRC-012 also states that the LRO mission and several agencies "have agreed to use a 0 to 360
    longitude domain".
  - SRC-013 says that ODE stores products in planetocentric, positive east, 0–360°.
- **ME frame definition (SRC-014):** "The +Z axis of this system is aligned with the north mean
  lunar rotation axis". The ME–PA rotation for DE421 is "approximately 0.0288473 degrees".
- **Assessment:** both domains are planetocentric and east-positive, so the app's provisional
  −180…+180 east-positive convention is consistent with SRC-012. Data from 0–360 sources needs a
  documented, tested conversion (longitude > 180 → longitude − 360). The convention is still
  **provisional** until the app's actual data sources are chosen.

## Conflicts and uncertainty

1. **Longitude domain.**
   - −180…180 east: SRC-005, SRC-007, SRC-001 and SRC-012.
   - 0…360 east: SRC-012 (the LRO agreement), SRC-013 and SRC-006.
   - SRC-008's bounding box spans −180…360.

   All are east-positive and planetocentric, so this is a range difference, not a direction
   conflict.

2. **Planetographic exceptions.** SRC-013 lists the Clementine HIRES mosaic as "Planetographic
   spherical coordinates with r = 1737.4 km". Data from such products must not be treated as
   planetocentric without checking.
3. **Reference radius.**
   - Most sources use 1737.4 km.
   - SRC-013 lists Lunar Prospector gravity data at r = 1738.0 km.
   - SRC-009's displacement files use a 1727400 m base with an offset, which is an encoding detail.
4. **Trek CRS code.** SRC-001 writes the ESRI code 104903 as an EPSG URN. Its definition comes
   only from a non-agency registry (SRC-004).
5. **USGS WMS CRS.** SRC-008 offers the Earth code `EPSG:4326` for lunar data, and the meaning of
   `IAU_2015:30100` was not verified.
6. **Licences.**
   - Trek (SRC-001 to SRC-003) and LROC (SRC-006) state no licence.
   - USGS products (SRC-005, SRC-007) say "public domain" with "Please cite authors".
   - SRC-010 is general NASA policy, not a Trek licence.
   - OPM (SRC-011) states none.
7. **Link stability.** The LROC WMS host listed by USGS has moved (it redirects to SRC-006), so
   expect link rot.
8. **Leaflet docs version.** SRC-016 was read as the current reference page, not confirmed as the
   1.9.x page. The EPSG4326 behaviour noted in `PROJECT_PLAN.md` was checked against the installed
   Leaflet 1.9.4 source code.

9. **IM-2 distance from its intended site.** SRC-027 says "more than 1,300 feet (400 meters)" in
   its text and "about 820 feet" in an image caption. Both are kept; the app does not give a
   distance.
10. **IM-1 end of operations and tilt.** SRC-023 gives Feb 29, 2024 as the _planned_ end; SRC-025
    says operations ceased "seven days after landing". A Feb 28 power-down and a ~30° tilt appear
    only on the NSSDCA page, which would not load (see below), so the app states neither.
11. **Chandrayaan-3 after the lunar night.** ISRO (SRC-032) last says efforts to make contact
    were underway; that no signal was received is reported by journalism (SRC-034) and is
    labelled so in the app.
12. **Gemini free tier for users in the EEA, UK and Switzerland.** The terms (SRC-037, effective
    2026-03-23) say only Paid Services may be used when making an API client available to users
    there. The billing FAQ (SRC-042) answers "Can I use the Gemini API for free in EEA (including
    EU), the UK, and CH?" with "Yes, we make the free tier and paid tier available in many
    regions." Both are kept; the FAQ may be about the developer, the terms about the app's users.
    Not resolved (KNOWN_ISSUES KI-016).

13. **LuSEE-Night battery mass.** 50 kg for 7160 Wh (SRC-046, July 2024) against "∼40 kg"
    (SRC-047, January 2023). Both are pre-launch design values; the newer one is used for the
    Power check's 143 Wh/kg, which is labelled as derived from it.
14. **Battery specific energy: cell or pack.** SRC-045's 150–270 Wh/kg is for commercial cells;
    its pack table spans 57.75–250 Wh/kg with no stated range. 143 Wh/kg (SRC-046) lies inside
    the pack table's span; using it for every mission is a team assumption.
15. **Night length.** LuSEE-Night plans for 328 h (SRC-046); the team's power lab file uses
    354 h (half of 29.53 days). The app uses neither: it measures the longest dark stretch at
    the place from its own Sun calculation.

16. **Chandrayaan-3 coordinates (resolved 2026-11-13).** data/sites.json now uses LROC's observed
    site, 69.3741° S, 32.32° E (SRC-060, Ayah). ISRO (SRC-033) gives the _planned_ prime landing
    site, 69.367621° S, 32.348126° E, about 0.36 km away (derived); the app shows that note under
    the coordinates. The plan's earlier 69.373° S, 32.319° E is no longer used.
17. **IM-2 coordinates (resolved 2026-11-13).** data/sites.json now uses 84.7906° S, 29.1957° E
    (SRC-058, Ayah), replacing the plan's rounded 84.79° S, 29.20° E; the team data sheet (docs/team)
    gives the same values. Ayah's Horizons exports (SRC-056) used 29.19567, −84.79059 (Sun) and
    29.2, −84.79 (Earth): within a few tens of metres, negligible for the check.
18. **Nova-C battery.** 1554 Wh from Intuitive Machines' FCC filing (SRC-064, tag S, pre-launch),
    against 700 Wh (25 Ah at 28 V) on a secondary web page (Miral's earlier sheet, tag S\*). The app
    uses 1554 Wh and shows the 700 Wh conflict note next to it. Not resolved.
19. **Nova-C solar power.** A maximum of 788 W from three body panels in Intuitive Machines' FCC
    filing (SRC-064, tag S), against 200 W on a secondary page (SRC-065, Wikipedia, tag S\*). Since
    Miral's update of 2026-11-13 the app shows 788 W (the opened source wins, as for the battery)
    with the 200 W conflict note. It feeds no verdict. Not resolved.
20. **Blue Ghost 1 solar power.** Up to 400 W on Firefly's mission page (SRC-066, tag S); Miral's
    note says other pages give 400 to 650 W (those pages are not identified). The app shows 400 W
    with the note. Not resolved.
21. **Facts in data/landers.json notes without a recorded link.** Nova-C's 100 W night load rests on
    Intuitive Machines saying IM-1 had roughly 10 to 20 hours of battery left after sunset
    ("Reuters, Feb 2024"; no URL); the 700 Wh figure in conflict 18 comes from an unnamed secondary
    page; Griffin-1's name says "launch reported late 2026 to early 2027" with no source (not shown in the app: no site
    uses Griffin-1). Ask Miral for the links before relying on them.

22. **"Visible" in PGDA's illumination maps vs the app.** SRC-069/SRC-070 count the Sun or Earth as
    visible when any part of its disc is unobscured; the app's checks use the centre. Values from
    those maps (e.g. Light %) would come out equal to or higher than a centre-based figure and must
    be labelled with their own definition, not presented as the same measure. They also rest on
    older elevation maps (LDEM_85S_060M, LDEM_75S_120M) than the app's terrain (SRC-048).
